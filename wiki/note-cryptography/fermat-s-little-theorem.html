Fermat's Little Theorem.

Theorem. Let G\mathbb{G}G be a finite group of order m=∣G∣m=|\mathbb{G}|m=∣G∣. Then for any g∈Gg\in \mathbb{G}g∈G, it holds that gm=1g^m=1gm=1.

We could see the similarity between the proof of Fermat's little theorem and Euler's theorem. In fact, Fermat's little theorem is the general form of Euler's theorem. Let G\mathbb{G}G be the modulo multiplication group (Z/n)∗(\mathbb{Z}/n)^{*}(Z/n)∗, one could derive the Euler's theorem.
