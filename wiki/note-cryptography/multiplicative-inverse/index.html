乘法逆元

2023.06.02

Multiplicative Inverse.

Definition. If for a given integer bbb, there exists an integer ccc such that bc=1mod  Nbc=1\mod{N}bc=1modN, we say that bbb is invertible modulo NNN, and call ccc a multiplicative inverse of bbb modulo NNN.

The multiplicative inverse is NOT unique. For an invertible bbb, if ccc is an inverse of bbb, every xxx congruent with ccc modulo NNN is also an inverse of bbb. (x≡cmod  Nx\equiv c\mod{N}x≡cmodN). We use b−1b^{-1}b−1 to denote the inverse of bbb that b−1∈{1,2,...N−1}b^{-1}\in\{1,2,...N-1\}b−1∈{1,2,...N−1}.

Theorem. Let b,Nb,Nb,N be integers, with b≥1b\geq 1b≥1, and N>1N>1N>1. Then bbb is invertible modulo NNN if and only if bbb is relatively prime to NNN. Note that integers b,Nb,Nb,N are relatively prime (or coprime) means gcd⁡(b,N)=1\gcd(b,N)=1gcd(b,N)=1.

Therefore, for a prime ppp, every b∈{1,2,...p−1}b\in \{1,2,...p-1\}b∈{1,2,...p−1} is invertible modulo ppp.

The above theorem could be proved by applying **Bezout's identity: ** it states that for every positive integer a,ba,ba,b, there exists a pair of integers ⟨X,Y⟩\langle X,Y \rangle⟨X,Y⟩ that Xa+Yb=gcd⁡(a,b)Xa+Yb=\gcd(a,b)Xa+Yb=gcd(a,b).

Eureka moment!

逆元这个概念算是老熟人了；它将模意义下的除法转化为乘法。
